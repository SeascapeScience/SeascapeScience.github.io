--- layout: archive title: "An Atlas of Radical Oceanography" permalink: /portfolio/ author_profile: true --- Do you appear on the map? Do you want to? A map is a story told by a mapmaker. Like other stories, maps are made of language and built on a history of previous stories told over time. They have characters and settings. Maps can convey, amuse, educate, and persuade. They can reveal or obscure secrets. They also carry our biases and agendas, often with messages and subtext that even the mapmaker might not be aware of. And like stories, they carry power. The ocean is largely invisible to humanity. Instead of seeing it directly, we view it through the lens of data and algorithms. The resulting maps are largely shaped by who has decided to collect data, and who has decided to map it and how. Some characters and their narratives are highly visible, while others are invisible, and this visibility or invisibility can be beneficial or harmful, depending on the circumstances. Counter-mapping can retell old narratives, revealing and shaping issues of justice, representation, access, and equity. This is a collection of ocean counter-maps I am starting to assemble.
